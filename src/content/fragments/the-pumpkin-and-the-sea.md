Yayoi Kusama's yellow pumpkin sits at the end of a wooden dock, looking out to sea. It is covered in black polka dots. It is enormous. It is also, somehow, exactly the right thing.

Naoshima was a fishing island in slow decline. The sea got overfished. The young left. The old stayed. Then Benesse Corporation and architect Tadao Ando arrived in the 1980s with a project: turn the island into an open-air museum.

The Chichu Art Museum is buried underground so it doesn't damage the landscape. You walk through concrete corridors carved into a hillside to see three Claude Monet water lily paintings in a room with no artificial lighting — only skylights that change the canvases with the time of day.

I sat in that room for forty minutes. I watched the light change. Nobody spoke.

The pumpkin was waiting at the dock when I came back down to take the ferry. It had been swept into the sea by a typhoon in 2021 and brought back. Restored, replaced, still here.

I thought: that is what this whole island is. Swept away, brought back. Still here.
