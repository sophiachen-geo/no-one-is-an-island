The village was founded by Hakka people in the late 18th century. They made salt — the island's name means *salt field*. Catholic missionaries arrived in the 19th century and, over two generations, converted the entire population. By 1875, Yim Tin Tsai was the only fully Catholic village in Hong Kong.

By 1998, it was empty. The last families had left.

St. Joseph's Chapel, built in 1890, survived. It was restored in 2003, won a UNESCO heritage award, and now receives visitors by ferry from Sai Kung on weekends. The salt pans have been restored too. You can watch volunteers harvest sea salt the old way.

The stone houses stand empty. Their doorways frame rectangles of blue sky.

Walking through the village alone, between the silent houses and the chapel with its red roof, I felt the specific quality of a place that has been loved and left. Not ruins — loved. Still tended. But the people are gone, and what remains is their careful architecture and their faith, preserved like salt.
