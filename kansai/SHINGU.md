# SHINGU

**Spine:** Shingū exists because flows converge here. Water, timber, pilgrims, trade, roads, railways and regional services accumulated between mountain, river and sea. Yet the same geography that made the city possible also concentrates flood, tsunami, slope and accessibility risk. Shingū therefore cannot make itself resilient simply by keeping hazards out. Its resilience lies in layering protection, adapting occupation, preserving routes of escape, creating redundancy and preparing for failure across the city and the wider watershed.

WHY HERE → WHY DANGEROUS → HOW SHINGŪ ADAPTS → WHERE THE STRATEGY BREAKS → KAMIKURA AS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT IN MINIATURE

That gives you a movement from region → city → system → contradiction → street corner.

The Kumano River is simultaneously a hazard system, ecological system, transportation corridor and sacred cultural landscape. MLIT's basin-resilience project explicitly calls for green infrastructure that harmonises with the history and culture of the World Heritage Kumano pilgrimage landscape, including river-mouth tidal flats, gravel bars, historical scenery and forest functions. KKR Japan

So the objective is not:

nature outside → city inside → barrier between them.

It is closer to:

watershed → settlement → infrastructure → ecology → culture → behaviour, all interacting.

That is why Shingū is such a good case for your project. The city cannot sever itself from the flows that threaten it without also severing itself from the flows that make it Shingū.

## Six themes: where the existing evidence goes (plan of 3 October 2026)

Yes. I’d sort the existing evidence **much more aggressively** than before. The six themes are good, but they should not each become giant buckets. Each piece of evidence needs a **primary home**, and only a few should reappear elsewhere as a reference.

There is also one important structural correction: some of your existing evidence does **not** belong inside Themes 1–6 at all. The basin-scale material, demographic contraction, and regional-centrality evidence should form a short **prologue**, because those establish why Shingū matters before you zoom into morphology. Likewise, the methods/QA material should sit underneath the thematic structure rather than becoming part of it.

#### And I would change the order slightly

Your six-theme sequence is basically right, but the strongest overall narration is:

**PROLOGUE — Why this city matters**  
regional node + shrinking population + mountain/river/sea setting

**1. GROUND — What structures space?**  
terrain → water → bridges → parcels → section

**2. TIME — What survives change?**  
aerials → inherited alignments → railway → reconstruction → ghost morphology

**3. HAZARD — How does disturbance produce form?**  
Kawaraya → four hazards → infrastructure → evacuation → compact planning

**4. FLOWS — What makes the city operate?**  
water → pilgrimage → rail/road → network vulnerability → bicycle → regional/watershed relations

**5. EVERYDAY — How are these systems actually inhabited?**  
houses → bridges → temple → school → library → park → sacred/ordinary continuity

**6. REPRESENTATION — Why have we not usually seen the city this way?**  
tourist map → photography → literature → GIS → your own field gaze

That last transition is important.

Theme 6 should make the reader realize:

**“Ah. All the drains, bridges, schools, parcels and ordinary houses were there all along. They disappeared because conventional representations of Kumano trained me to look somewhere else.”**

Then your enormous amount of technical evidence is no longer tangential. It becomes almost the methodological answer to the representational problem:

**GIS showed one system.  
The cadastral map showed another.  
The DEM showed another.  
Historical aerials showed persistence.  
Network analysis showed movement.  
Field cycling showed lived permeability.  
Photographs showed what official imagery overlooks.**

That, I think, is the point at which this stops being merely a very sophisticated Shingū case study and becomes a coherent argument about **how to read a city whose terrain, risk, sacred geography, infrastructure and ordinary life have been layered together for centuries.**
